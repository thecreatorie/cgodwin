Greeting

Greeting is the first thing a guest meets, and it sets the temperature for everything that follows. Caroline builds her own costumes, so a greeter can be created to a brand, a theme or a colour palette rather than pulled from a rental rack — working the door, the queue line, the red carpet or the room.

What's in the Set

Characters & Greeting

01

Door & Entrance Greeting

A costumed character stationed at the entrance to welcome guests as they arrive. The cheapest way to make an ordinary doorway feel like the start of something.

02

Meet-and-Greet & Photo Moments

Any character can be staffed as a photo op with a handler and a queue line, so guests arrive, pose, post and move along.

03

Roaming & Walkabout

Working the room rather than a fixed post — mingling, posing, and reappearing through the night in different looks.

04

Creature & Alien Characters

Full-body alien, lizard and lizard-alien looks with body paint and prosthetic styling. Genuinely unsettling in the best way.

05

Nature & Whimsy

Mushroom, flower, butterfly — soft, family-friendly, and the best greeters for daytime events and photo queues.

06

Story Characters

Cheshire Cat, Sally, tiger, disco dancer and devil — recognisable looks for themed events.

07

Cyberpunk & LED Characters

Illuminated futuristic looks for club and after-dark greeting.

08

Aerial & Stilt Greeting

A greeter taken up — on stilts through the crowd, or a character in the air. The alien in the aerial sphere is the strongest example.

09

Brand Ambassador & Sampling

Product handling, flyering or sampling built into the greet, so the character earns its place on the budget twice.

Where & How It Happens

Booking Greeting & Characters

Entrances, doors & red carpets

The default booking — a character posted where guests arrive, for a set number of hours.

Themed parties & galas

Halloween, immersive events, brand worlds, private estates.

Festivals & immersive venues

AREA15, art festivals, Burning Man camps and art cars.

Family & daytime events

Nature and whimsy characters are built for kids, parades and daytime activations.

Brand activations & trade shows

A greeter built in the brand's palette, driving booth traffic and handling product.

Content shoots

Character looks photograph extremely well and are frequently booked for stills rather than live audiences.

What the venue needs to provide

Figures tagged Caroline to confirm are drafted from standard industry practice and still need to be checked against her actual equipment before this page goes to a client.

Booking length
Greeting and walkabout are booked in 45-minute sets with 15-minute breaks; heavy prosthetic looks need shorter sets Caroline to confirm
Green room
Private, with a mirror, a chair, water, and access to a sink for body paint
Get-in time
Body-paint and prosthetic characters need roughly 90 minutes to 2 hours before doors Caroline to confirm
Temperature
Full-coverage costumes overheat fast — shade and a cool-down spot are needed above about 90 °F Caroline to confirm
Stilt characters
Add every requirement listed on the Stilt Walking page
Handler
Recommended for dense crowds and for any character with restricted vision
Multiple looks
Two characters in one booking need a green room and roughly 20 minutes between changes Caroline to confirm
